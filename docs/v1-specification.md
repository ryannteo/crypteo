# Crypteo V1 Behavioral Specification

This is the authoritative specification of current V1 behavior at HEAD. The
[architecture overview](architecture.md) explains component boundaries; the
[data pipeline](data-pipeline.md) defines acquisition and storage semantics.
Durable architectural changes require an [ADR](decisions/README.md).

The implementation provides deterministic scanner-only replay, seven market
research tools, and bounded opportunity-led model investigation, thesis, critique,
and final assessment through LangGraph, with optional durable SQLite lifecycle
journaling and immutable terminal research artifacts. One historical replay
lifecycle supports the deterministic baseline, fresh model research with mandatory
durable journaling, and frozen validated-research-artifact reproduction. A paired
baseline/research comparison artifact validates and aggregates single-candidate
windows; mechanical window planning and a resumable evaluation runner with frozen
reproduction are implemented, while statistics, reports, and multi-candidate
windows remain unimplemented.

## V1 Scope and Market Environment

- Coinbase Exchange spot: `BTC-USD`, `ETH-USD`, and `SOL-USD`.
- Completed 1-hour OHLCV candles on a timezone-aware UTC timeline.
- One deterministic scanner; LONG simulation with one pending or open trade.
- Market-derived evidence only; no predictive support/resistance or volatility
  percentile/regime labels.
- Research contracts distinguish `TRADE`, `NO_TRADE`, and `ABSTAIN`;
  deterministic risk rejection is a separate outcome.
- No real orders, trading credentials, private keys, or withdrawal capabilities.

## Hard Responsibility Boundary

Deterministic Python owns market calculations, scanning, trade terms, risk
estimation and sizing, simulation, accounting, temporal access, replay, and metrics.
The bounded LLM workflow selects tools and interprets evidence, constructs and
critiques theses, and forms qualitative research assessments. A `TRADE` assessment
cannot authorize execution or supply calculated financial values.

`TradeTerms` does not automatically enter the model-facing schema. Any future
candidate-level references require an explicit research-contract change and must
refer to runtime-owned deterministic contracts. See
[ADR-001](decisions/ADR-001-deterministic-vs-llm-boundary.md).

## Market Contracts

Frozen `Candle` records use finite Decimal prices and volume (rejecting binary
float inputs), strict aware UTC timestamps, hourly open/close boundaries, one-hour
intervals, positive OHLC prices, nonnegative volume, and consistent high/low bounds.
Availability cannot precede completion but need not be hourly aligned. Python
inputs cannot coerce strings or numbers into timestamps. Dataset manifests use
schema version `"1"` and strict nonnegative row counts, with validated coverage
and gap relationships. Canonical identity and acquisition validation are defined
in the [data pipeline](data-pipeline.md).

## Opportunity Contract

An `Opportunity` is the small deterministic research-candidate contract for opportunity-led research. It contains only `opportunity_id`, `venue`, `pair`, `timeframe`, `observed_at`, `available_at`, `trigger_type`, `trigger_payload`, and `summary`.

`opportunity_id` is assigned by deterministic runtime or scanner logic, is never generated by the LLM, has no random schema default, and is stable enough for research, replay, journaling, and evaluation to reference. V1 uses the canonical Coinbase Exchange spot venue, supported `BTC-USD`, `ETH-USD`, and `SOL-USD` pairs, and one-hour timeframe. The scanner’s concrete identity encoding is defined under [Scanner Lock](#scanner-lock).

`observed_at` is when the deterministic trigger became complete. For the V1 hourly scanner this is the trigger candle's completion, not its open; for a window-derived trigger it is the latest point required for the trigger to be valid. `available_at` is the earliest time the opportunity could legitimately have been known, and the schema enforces only `available_at >= observed_at`.

`trigger_type` is an open-ended, stable, nonblank deterministic trigger identifier rather than an enumeration of scanner strategies. `trigger_payload` contains compact JSON-compatible machine-readable trigger facts with string mapping keys and no arbitrary Python objects. Complete histories, large indicator windows, raw datasets, and provider responses remain external. `summary` is a concise deterministic explanation of why the candidate exists.

An `Opportunity` is not an LLM decision, directional vote, order, execution or risk authorization, or portfolio authorization. It contains no run or decision identity, model-generated IDs or timestamps, scanner implementation/configuration objects, position or trade-level arithmetic, journal/graph metadata, LLM reasoning, thesis, or critique content.

## Evidence Contract

Every normalized `EvidenceItem` contains `evidence_id`, category/type, source, `observed_at`, `available_at`, provenance, tool/query parameters, dataset/source version, immutable structured payload or artifact reference, concise relevance, and optional quality metadata. Large dataframes and raw datasets stay outside graph state. Research capabilities emit this evidence rather than independent `BUY`, `SELL`, `BULLISH`, or `BEARISH` votes; the integrated research process owns interpretation.

Tool runtime owns authoritative time, fixed venue/timeframe, and source bindings.
Research tool arguments select a supported pair and, for relative strength, an
explicit benchmark; callers cannot supply a cutoff or window override. Results
expose temporal provenance and typed unavailability. The thesis and critic stages
consume evidence contracts rather than market-provider objects.

`Opportunity` and `EvidenceItem` schemas enforce internal timestamp consistency, including `available_at >= observed_at`. They do not enforce `available_at <= SimulationClock.now`; `SimulationClock` and `PointInTimeDataGateway` own authoritative visibility at replay time.
Common research contracts revalidate existing and nested model instances and
detach mutable input collections into deeply immutable validated state.

## Deterministic Market Research

`MarketResearchService` is implemented in
[market_research.py](../src/crypteo/market_research.py), with immutable typed
[contracts and payloads](../src/crypteo/domain/market_research.py) and private
[research arithmetic](../src/crypteo/domain/_market_research_arithmetic.py).
The fixed policy is `market_research_v1`.

### Public API and assembly

The service exposes `trend(pair)`, `momentum(pair)`, `volatility(pair)`,
`bollinger_position(pair)`, `levels(pair)`, `volume_context(pair)`, and
`relative_strength(pair, benchmark)`. Each returns
`ResearchToolSuccess | ResearchToolUnavailable`. Supported markets are Coinbase
`BTC-USD`, `ETH-USD`, and `SOL-USD`, using completed 1h candles. Tools require no
`Opportunity` and expose no timestamp, arbitrary lookback, or window override,
allowing reuse by opportunity-led research and future trader-led orchestration.

`build_market_research(clock, datasets)` validates canonical datasets and any
supplied acquisition linkage, then constructs the gateway and source-provenance
bindings from the same authoritative inputs. It accepts canonical or historical
dataset objects, with one binding per supplied pair; duplicate bindings raise.
The supplied clock remains runtime-owned. Direct service construction is a runtime
integration seam whose source bindings must describe the gateway's datasets.
Raw-capture verification remains at the acquisition/publication boundary.
The model-backed research workflow consumes this service; scanner-only replay
does not invoke the tools or workflow.

### Fixed capabilities, windows, and formulas

Let `T` be captured gateway time. `Ck`, `Hk`, `Lk`, and `Vk` denote close, high,
low, and base-asset volume of the candle ending at `T - k hours`; `C0` is the
current completed close. All windows below are exact contiguous hourly windows
ending at `T`, including the current candle. Returns and distances are fractions.

| Capability | Required bars | Fixed measurements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trend | 50 | `SMA20 = mean(C0..C19)`; `SMA50 = mean(C0..C49)`; `close_vs_sma20 = C0 / SMA20 - 1`; `close_vs_sma50 = C0 / SMA50 - 1`; `sma20_vs_sma50 = SMA20 / SMA50 - 1`. |
| Momentum | 25 | Simple returns `return_1h = C0 / C1 - 1`, `return_6h = C0 / C6 - 1`, `return_24h = C0 / C24 - 1`; `previous_return_6h = C6 / C12 - 1`; `return_change_6h = return_6h - previous_return_6h`. |
| Volatility | 41 | `TRk = max(Hk - Lk, abs(Hk - C(k+1)), abs(Lk - C(k+1)))`. Current mean is `mean(TR0..TR19)`; previous mean is `mean(TR20..TR39)`, seeded by `C40`. Normalize current mean by `C0` and previous mean by `C20`; `normalized_range_ratio = current_normalized_range / previous_normalized_range`. |
| Bollinger position | 20 | Scanner-identical SMA20 and population standard deviation `σ` over `C0..C19`; `lower = SMA20 - 2σ`, `upper = SMA20 + 2σ`; `percent_b = (C0 - lower) / (upper - lower)`; `bandwidth_fraction = (upper - lower) / SMA20`. |
| Recent levels | 25 | `recent_high = max(H1..H24)` and `recent_low = min(L1..L24)`, excluding the current candle. `close_vs_high = C0 / recent_high - 1`; `close_vs_low = C0 / recent_low - 1`. |
| Volume context | 25 | Current base-asset volume `V0`, previous 24-bar mean `mean(V1..V24)`, and `volume_ratio = V0 / mean(V1..V24)`. |
| Relative strength | 25 per market | Subject and benchmark simple returns `C0 / C6 - 1` and `C0 / C24 - 1`; each spread is subject return minus benchmark return at the same horizon. |

Volatility reuses existing `mean_true_range` for two non-overlapping sets of 20
true ranges; the shared candle ending at `T - 20h` supplies the current set's
previous-close seed and the previous set's ending close. These are arithmetic
means, not Wilder ATR, a volatility percentile, or a regime classification.
Bollinger `percent_b` is unclipped and can lie below zero or above one.

Recent levels select the most recent prior candle when extrema tie and retain
its close time. The payload names for `close_vs_high` and `close_vs_low` are
`recent_high_distance` and `recent_low_distance`, respectively. These are
observational levels, not predictive support/resistance or proposed trade terms.

Relative strength requires explicit ordered subject/benchmark roles and exactly
aligned windows. All six ordered comparisons are supported: BTC/ETH, BTC/SOL,
ETH/BTC, ETH/SOL, SOL/BTC, and SOL/ETH (each asset's USD pair). Self-comparison
raises; no default benchmark is inferred.

### Temporal boundary and unavailable outcomes

One authoritative `PointInTimeDataGateway` supplies all research history. Each
call captures `T = gateway.now`, which must be a timezone-aware hourly UTC
boundary. Required coverage is `[T - N hours, T)`, with the final candle ending
at `T`. Every contributing candle must be completed and visible:
`close_time <= T` and `available_at <= T`. The service does not advance time,
and checks gateway time after each market read; clock movement during cross-asset
reads raises rather than combining observations from different boundaries.

There are no shortened windows, older-history fallback, interpolation,
forward-fill, or latest-common-timestamp alignment. Expected absence produces
`ResearchToolUnavailable`, retaining query, policy, required window, affected
subject/benchmark role, and one reason:

| Reason | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` | A required market has no bound source. |
| `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE` | Visible history is empty or lacks the candle ending at `T`. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY` | The reference exists, but visible history begins after the required window start. |
| `HISTORY_GAP` | History reaches the required start and reference, but a required hourly slot is missing or delayed beyond `T`. |

Source bindings are checked first. For bound markets, all returned histories are
revalidated before missing-history classification; per-market checks follow the
table's reference, leading-history, then gap order, subject before benchmark.
Malformed, corrupt, future/invisible, mismatched, duplicate, or unordered gateway
data raises rather than becoming ordinary unavailability.

Availability is derived as the maximum availability of **all** contributing
candles across all inputs, including previous-close seeds. Since success requires
the visible candle ending at `T`, successful V1 evidence naturally satisfies
`observed_at == available_at == T`; this is not permission to ignore dependencies.

### Evidence normalization, identity, and provenance

The existing `EvidenceItem` remains the common envelope. `ResearchToolSuccess`
contains typed context, a capability-specific payload, and its normalized evidence.
Contracts validate fixed window geometry, source roles, payload relationships,
envelope linkage, and evidence identity, including nested and JSON revalidation.
The service establishes the actual visible candle dependencies; schema validation
alone does not prove market provenance.

Successful tools emit interpretation-neutral market evidence, with no
bullish/bearish labels, recommendation, confidence, weighting, or
`TRADE` / `NO_TRADE` / `ABSTAIN` assessment. Evidence quality is unset. Typed
context is embedded in `provenance.query_params`, and the deterministic payload
is normalized into the immutable envelope. Raw candle windows stay outside it.

Semantic `evidence_id` is a SHA-256 digest of identity encoding version `1`,
the `market_research_v1` policy and capability, ordered subject/benchmark roles
and market identities (venue, pair, timeframe), observed time, required-window
geometry, canonical digests of the actual contributing candle windows, and the
normalized deterministic payload. Each window digest includes encoding version,
market identity, window geometry, and complete contributing candle records,
including OHLCV, open/close times, and availability timestamps. Encoding uses sorted,
compact UTF-8 JSON, normalized exact Decimal strings, and UTC timestamps with
microseconds.

Semantic identity excludes full dataset version, acquisition ID, paths, invocation
metadata, and prose. Changing only data after `T`, or older noncontributing data,
does not change point-in-time evidence identity. Acquisition-only differences also
leave it unchanged. Changing any contributing candle changes its window digest
and evidence identity even when the numerical payload remains unchanged.

Dataset and acquisition identities remain explicit provenance in each input's
source reference, alongside role, venue, pair, timeframe, source, and source schema
version; acquisition ID may be absent. Relative-strength provenance preserves
both subject and benchmark roles. `provenance.source_version` is canonical source
schema version `1`, not dataset content identity or research-policy version.
`provenance.producer` identifies `market_research_v1.<capability>`.

### Numerical policy

Research arithmetic uses deterministic Decimal operations in a private
50-significant-digit, half-even context, independent of ambient Decimal settings
and without binary floats. Scanner Bollinger/SMA20 arithmetic is reused;
existing `mean_true_range` retains its exact arithmetic rather than being rounded
to a new research mean. Calculations and relational validators share the numerical
policy.

Zero denominators produce a `ResearchRatio` with `value=None` and an explicit
`ZERO_PREVIOUS_NORMALIZED_RANGE`, `ZERO_BAND_WIDTH`, or `ZERO_BASELINE_VOLUME`
reason as appropriate. They do not make otherwise valid evidence unavailable.
Zero numerators with positive denominators produce defined zero ratios; prices
and price means used as denominators must remain positive.

## Temporal and Replay Invariants

`SimulationClock` advances explicitly with strict UTC timestamps, permits the
same time, and rejects backward movement. The gateway reconstructs and validates
every `Candle` instance before indexing, rejects duplicate market/open identities,
and detaches its sorted history from the caller's collection. `read_candles`
accepts market identity and returns only candles with both
`close_time <= clock.now` and `available_at <= clock.now`; it exposes no cutoff
argument or clock mutation. Future records stay hidden rather than invalidating
the stored history. All timestamps are aware UTC.

Historical consumers use this gateway. Acquisition and persistence can inspect
full datasets for integrity, but cannot grant historical visibility. A decision
after close T is eligible for the next bar open at T; financial recording waits
for completed evidence. No model may choose or extend the cutoff. Decision-time
retrieval of prior theses, decisions, or outcomes is prohibited. Durable research
journal access supports auditing and inspection, never decision memory.

Tests deliberately seed future data and require it to remain unreachable. Prompts are not a security control.

## Scanner Lock

`OpportunityScanner.scan(pair=...)` returns every qualifying opportunity in the
current gateway-visible snapshot, in chronological order, with no delivery state.
Repeated calls may return the same opportunities; replay owns run-local delivery.

For each 20-close contiguous window, including the current candle:
`SMA20 = sum(closes) / 20`, `variance = sum((close - SMA20)^2) / 20`,
`σ = sqrt(variance)`, and `lower_band = SMA20 - 2σ`. Calculation uses a private
50-digit, half-even Decimal context, independent of caller settings. Mixed markets,
duplicate timestamps, and reversed ordering raise. Missing hours reset warm-up;
statistics are `None` until 20 contiguous bars exist again. Pure analytics accepts
supplied candles; callers establish visibility through the gateway.

A crossover requires the previous close at/above its own lower band and the
current close below its own lower band. The first valid band only initializes
comparison, so the earliest opportunity is candle 21. `None`/gaps reset comparison.
Continued below-band closes do not emit; equality or an above-band close re-arms.
Observation time is the trigger close; availability is the maximum availability
across both compared windows, including all dependencies.

The trigger type is `bollinger_lower_cross_v1`. IDs are `sha256:` plus the digest
of sorted compact UTF-8 JSON containing trigger type, venue, pair, timeframe, and
observed time (UTC `Z`, six fractional digits). Payload, availability, and summary
are excluded from identity. Compact payloads retain previous/current closes and
lower bands. Newly visible internal candles can change snapshot results.

The transparent, untuned rule scales its band with observed volatility and supplies
comparable candidates without separate scanners for research and baseline selection. It can identify extremes in both ranges and
selloffs; candidate generation makes no claim that buying is appropriate. See
[ADR-004](decisions/ADR-004-scanner-snapshot-semantics.md) for snapshot rationale.

## Canonical Historical Data

Canonical inputs contain validated hourly candles, actual coverage, internal gaps,
and semantic dataset identity. Requested completeness and raw observations retain
separate acquisition/capture identities. There is no synthesized or forward-filled
OHLCV. Coinbase normalization assigns candle close as `available_at`; acquisition
time is separate provenance. See the [data pipeline guide](data-pipeline.md) for
the full normalization, identity, publication, and loading contract.

## Decision and Risk Outcomes

`TradeDecision` is the final LLM-facing research assessment. It contains qualitative rationale, evidence and counterevidence references, confidence, uncertainties, critic response, abstention reasons, and exactly one of `TRADE|NO_TRADE|ABSTAIN`. It does not own runtime/journal identity, authoritative timestamps, financial arithmetic, execution fields, or references to deterministic trade-level/template contracts. The implemented `TradeTerms` contract remains runtime-owned and is not part of this model-facing schema.

- `TRADE`: research supports further consideration of the candidate LONG setup. It is not an order, execution authorization, risk approval, sizing approval, or portfolio authorization.
- `NO_TRADE`: Crypteo has enough evidence to actively reject the candidate. It is a substantive research conclusion, not an abstention.
- Model `ABSTAIN`: completed research cannot form a sufficiently reliable conclusion because evidence is insufficient, conflicting, stale, unavailable, or unreliable. Standalone interrupted research also uses runtime `ABSTAIN` with a typed operational failure; historical replay rejects that interrupted artifact and aborts evaluation.
- `RISK_REJECTED`: deterministic authorization refused a proposal. It is a downstream risk outcome that the LLM cannot emit.

The research schema keeps `TRADE`, `NO_TRADE`, and `ABSTAIN` distinct. Historical research replay admits only validated `COMPLETED / MODEL` assessments. A `TRADE` outcome permits runtime-owned deterministic terms generation, construction of a `TradeProposal` from those frozen terms, and `RiskService` authorization. `NO_TRADE` and model `ABSTAIN` prohibit financial preparation; operational research failure aborts evaluation. Authorization supplies an authorized `OrderIntent` for simulated processing or yields `RISK_REJECTED`. `RiskService` does not directly consume `TradeDecision`. The LLM does not generate stop/target levels, quantity, or other financial terms; deterministic code supplies the levels and the risk service computes quantity.

## Deterministic Trade Terms

The stateless `TradeTermsService.generate(pair=...)` reads through the authoritative point-in-time gateway. Generation reads `gateway.now`, never accepts a caller/model cutoff, and never advances the clock. It requires exactly 21 contiguous visible completed hourly candles spanning `[gateway.now - 21h, gateway.now)`, with the last candle ending at `gateway.now`. Every required candle must be available by that time; older history cannot replace a missing or delayed candle.

The current V1 policy is:

- The first candle supplies only the previous close. For each of the remaining 20 candles, true range is `max(high - low, abs(high - previous_close), abs(low - previous_close))`.
- `mean_true_range` is the arithmetic mean of those 20 true ranges, computed exactly without recursive smoothing.
- The reference is the last candle's close; raw stop = reference close - 2 × mean_true_range.
- Raw target = SMA20 of the last 20 closes, including the reference close, reusing the scanner's private 50-digit, half-even SMA calculation.
- Both raw levels are aligned downward to the configured price increment using exact integer-ratio flooring, including increments that are not powers of ten. Raw and aligned levels must each satisfy `0 < stop < reference < target`.

Generation fails closed with `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `HISTORY_UNAVAILABLE`, `ZERO_MEAN_TRUE_RANGE`, `INVALID_RAW_STOP`, `INVALID_RAW_TARGET`, or `INVALID_ALIGNED_LEVELS`. It does not shorten the window, substitute older candles, clamp invalid stops, or raise collapsed targets. Malformed inputs raise validation errors rather than ordinary rejection outcomes.

Successful terms are immutable and retain decision time, market identity, configuration, reference, derived statistics, and aligned levels. Their contract recomputes the levels; the service establishes the statistics' market provenance and temporal visibility. In scanner-only replay, terms are frozen before selection, authorization, and execution so selection cannot change the trade being evaluated. In model-backed replay, research precedes generation, and only validated model `TRADE` permits terms; terms are still deterministic and frozen before proposal/risk/execution. Generation does not imply research approval, portfolio sizing, execution authority, or automatic LLM exposure. See [ADR-007](decisions/ADR-007-deterministic-trade-terms.md) and its narrow ordering supersession in [ADR-011](decisions/ADR-011-model-research-historical-replay.md) for the durable boundaries; the multiplier, window, and target formula above are current V1 policy.

Replay implements opportunity freshness, deduplication, stale-term admission, replay orchestration and clock advancement, proposal construction from frozen terms, core metrics, and experiment manifests. The generator accepts a pair rather than an `Opportunity`; replay establishes whether a scanner candidate remains fresh.

## Scanner-Only Replay

`run_scanner_only(ReplayConfig, datasets, *, code=None) -> ReplayResult` implements
the scanner-only baseline. It validates exactly one explicitly supplied canonical
dataset per configured asset and preserves the complete supplied dataset identity.
It does not fetch, trim, repair, or enforce walk-forward splits. Existing artifacts
can be supplied through the [read-only canonical loader](data-pipeline.md#read-only-canonical-loading),
with optional explicit acquisition linkage. `ReplayConfig` requires UTC hourly
`start`/`end`, nonnegative `initial_cash`, a nonempty unique asset list, and
`trade_terms`, `risk`, and `execution` configurations. Terms and execution price
increments must match. `start == end` is valid: reconcile and observe once, with
no decision boundaries. An end before start is invalid.

One authoritative `SimulationClock` drives one gateway shared by the snapshot
scanner, terms service, risk service, and broker. At each hourly boundary in
`[start, end)`, replay reconciles the broker and records new lifecycle outcomes,
then scans every configured pair. Admission requires
`observed_at == gateway.now` and `available_at <= gateway.now`. Replay owns
delivery state; the scanner remains snapshot-based. First sightings receive
`ADMITTED`, `NOT_CURRENT`, or `NOT_AVAILABLE`; each disposition is terminal for
that run, so old/delayed candidates are never backdated or retried. Later sightings
receive `DUPLICATE` only after checking the canonical ADR-004
`Opportunity.identity_key`: trigger type (including version), venue, pair,
timeframe, and observed time. Conflicting reuse of an ID is an invariant failure.
Payload, summary, and availability changes alone do not change identity.

Candidates are processed in BTC-USD → ETH-USD → SOL-USD order, then by
`opportunity_id`, independent of input ordering. Pair priority is a temporary
allocation policy under the single-active-trade limitation. The durable boundary,
delivery, and allocation decisions are recorded in
[ADR-008](decisions/ADR-008-scanner-only-replay-boundary-delivery-ordering.md).

For each admitted candidate, replay freezes terms and verifies market identity,
configuration, and `terms.decision_at == opportunity.observed_at == gateway.now`.
Preparation still happens while busy or halted. Valid terms always produce a
baseline selection and deterministic proposal; there is no baseline `NO_TRADE`.
Risk checks the current portfolio, and each authorization is submitted immediately
before processing the next candidate, so later candidates see pending/open/halted
state. `TradeTermsRejection`, `RiskRejection`, `EntryRejection`, `DataGap`, `Fill`,
and `ClosedTrade` remain distinct typed outcomes. The boundary ends with a
portfolio/equity observation and an exact one-hour clock advance. At `end`, replay
reconciles and observes once,
without scanning, submitting, draining beyond the end, cancellation, or forced
liquidation. Positions still open after ordinary broker reconciliation remain
open and are marked when valid data exists. A normally completed result cannot
retain a pending order.

Runtime owns delivery state, order identities, and append-only in-memory record
sequence; the broker owns financial state. Records retain scan snapshots,
delivery dispositions, terms, baseline proposals, risk outcomes, submissions,
fills, closed trades, entry rejections, gaps, and boundary observations, reusing
domain payloads. A holding gap continues the schedule with unavailable valuation
and halted finances; subsequent valid proposals can receive `PORTFOLIO_HALTED`.
Malformed inputs, stale/mismatched terms, invariant violations, and unexpected
errors raise, with no partial result or recovery/persistence path. Scanner-only
replay lifecycle persistence and resumption are unimplemented. Scanner-only replay
does not invoke research tools, an LLM, or the `TradeDecision` workflow.

`ReplayResult` validates manifest/run identity, contiguous record sequences,
record/payload timing and linkage, the complete hourly observation schedule, and
agreement between final observation and portfolio. Its
[replay_validation.py](../src/crypteo/replay_validation.py) checks lifecycle
stage ordering, scan-to-delivery coverage and identity collisions, admitted
opportunity-to-terms/proposal/risk/submission linkage, and financial outcomes
against prior submissions and each observation's ledger prefix. It also checks
retained fills against execution configuration, STOP/TARGET exits against order
levels and provable same-candle facts, and state-based risk rejections against the
retained portfolio. These are artifact consistency checks, not a second execution
simulation or a reconstruction of market evidence. Validation, including JSON
deserialization, also recomputes all metrics with the canonical calculator and
requires exact equality with the supplied metrics. Immutable in-memory records
and these checks make serialized artifacts auditable without providing durable
storage or recovery.

`ExperimentManifest` contains schema version, `SCANNER_ONLY` mode, git commit and
deterministic source digest, Python/Pydantic versions, replay bounds/cash/ordered
assets, per-pair dataset/acquisition identities, explicit scanner policy, stable
terms policy and configuration, and risk/execution configurations. The default
source digest hashes relative names and contents of all Crypteo Python sources
and the checkout's `pyproject.toml`; docs, tests, absolute checkout paths, mtimes,
and bytecode do not participate. Outside a checkout, callers supply explicit
`CodeIdentity` with git commit and source digest. Canonical serialization uses
sorted compact UTF-8 JSON, normalized exact Decimal strings,
and UTC timestamps with microseconds. `run_id` hashes the manifest without itself;
order IDs hash `{run_id, opportunity_id, purpose: "order"}` independently of prior
success counts. Runtime owns both identities. Equivalent validated inputs and the
same manifest reproduce byte-stable results; there is no separate proposal ID.

Core metrics are starting/ending marked equity, realized net P&L and return,
marked return, maximum drawdown, entry/closed-trade counts, wins/losses/breakevens,
win rate, actual fees, coarse hourly exposure, STOP/TARGET/TIME counts,
`missing_entry_candle_count`, and completeness/unresolved-state indicators.
Open holdings use the gateway-visible completed close ending at the observation
boundary, with no hypothetical exit
costs. Drawdown is the maximum fractional decline from the running equity peak,
including initial cash. Returns, win rate, and exposure are fractions; ratio
outputs round half-even to 50 significant digits, independently of caller Decimal
context. Financial amounts remain exact.

Exposure counts each reconciled holding candle, including entry and exit candles,
divided by scheduled hours; it does not estimate intrabar duration. A holding gap
makes ending equity, marked return, full-period drawdown, and exposure unavailable.
Valid observations, prefix maximum drawdown, known exposure bars, actual fees,
and realized metrics remain available, with `valuation_valid_through` marking the
last valid prefix observation. Later candles do not resume or revalue a halted
position. `missing_entry_candle_count` counts terminal `MISSING_ENTRY_CANDLE`
entry rejections as foundational replay accounting; these leave no position and
do not themselves invalidate valuation.
Zero starting capital gives undefined returns; no closed trades gives undefined
win rate. Empty schedules reconcile/observe once and have undefined exposure;
flat/no-trade drawdown is zero. Financial `ReplayMetrics` are shared unchanged by
research replay; its additional factual metrics are defined below. Advanced
performance statistics remain deferred.

## Historical Research Replay

### Modes and public entrypoints

One shared historical lifecycle in [replay.py](../src/crypteo/replay.py) supports:

| Entrypoint/settings | Selection source | Manifest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synchronous `run_scanner_only` | Select every admitted candidate with valid deterministic terms | Schema `"1"`, `SCANNER_ONLY` |
| Async `run_research_replay`, `FRESH_INFERENCE` | New durable model research for every admitted candidate | Schema `"2"`, `RESEARCH` |
| Async `run_research_replay`, `FROZEN_RESEARCH` | Explicitly mapped, validated retained terminal research | Schema `"2"`, `RESEARCH` |

The async API in [research_replay.py](../src/crypteo/research_replay.py) is
`run_research_replay(config, datasets, *, research, store, model=None, code=None)`.
It returns a normally completed `ReplayResult` or raises; failures do not return
partial successful metrics. It requires a caller-owned `ResearchStore` in both
research modes. Configuration/dataset validation, full supplied dataset identity,
clock/gateway assembly, and optional explicit `CodeIdentity` follow baseline
semantics. The synchronous baseline remains usable inside an event loop without
research workflow, provider, or SQLite invocation. There is no research replay CLI.

`FreshResearchSettings` requires `configuration: ResearchWorkflowConfig`, a
nonblank `trial_id`, and strict positive integer `max_research_runs` (no default
unlimited cap). Fresh replay requires an `ObservableResearchModel` whose provider
matches configured model provider. It uses `run_durable_opportunity_research`;
in-memory-only research is not sufficient for replay admission. Publication is
followed by `store.load_terminal(research_run_id)` and comparison with the returned
artifact before replay consumes it.

`FrozenResearchSettings` requires the workflow `configuration` and explicit
`mapping`. Passing any model is forbidden. Frozen mode neither performs inference
nor runs LangGraph, invokes research tools, or constructs/invokes
`MarketResearchService` for research. It loads artifacts through `load_terminal`.
Deterministic scanning, terms after `TRADE`, execution, and valuation/accounting
still read point-in-time market data. Frozen mode is not globally free of
market-data reads.

### Shared lifecycle and candidate ordering

At boundary T:

1. Reconcile the broker and append newly known fills, entry rejections, closed
   trades, or a holding data gap.
2. If T equals `end`, record the final observation and finish; no scan or research.
3. Scan configured assets, sort candidates BTC-USD → ETH-USD → SOL-USD then
   `opportunity_id`, and record all delivery dispositions.
4. Process admitted candidates sequentially in that order.
5. Record the boundary portfolio/equity observation.
6. Advance exactly one hour, then reconcile the next boundary.

Freshness, availability, first-seen terminal delivery, duplicate identity checking,
scanning, valuation, and final-boundary semantics remain as specified for baseline.
No research runs for non-admitted deliveries or at the final boundary. An empty
interval reconciles/observes once and has zero research assessments.

Baseline preparation remains terms → baseline selection/proposal → risk →
authorized submission. Research preparation is opportunity → research → validated
artifact admission → outcome; only `COMPLETED / MODEL / TRADE` continues through
terms → `ResearchSelection` containing the deterministic `TradeProposal` → risk →
authorized submission. Research therefore precedes even a possible terms rejection.
Terms verify subject, configuration, and decision time at T and remain frozen
before proposal, authorization, and execution. The model supplies no stop, target,
quantity, risk authorization, execution price, fee, P&L, or portfolio mutation.

Each candidate completes research and optional financial preparation/submission
before the next candidate begins. There is no concurrent candidate research.
Risk sees the current portfolio: an earlier submission makes it pending for later
candidates at the same boundary, preserving the one-active-trade allocation order.
The model receives no portfolio state. All admitted candidates still undergo
research while busy or halted; model `TRADE` may subsequently receive
`PORTFOLIO_BUSY` or `PORTFOLIO_HALTED` from deterministic risk.

### Research conclusions and operational failure

| Admitted research | Meaning | Financial preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `COMPLETED / MODEL / TRADE` | Research supports further consideration of the LONG setup | Generate terms; valid terms produce proposal/risk; submit only if authorized |
| `COMPLETED / MODEL / NO_TRADE` | Substantive model rejection | No terms, risk authorization, or submission |
| `COMPLETED / MODEL / ABSTAIN` | Model could not form a sufficiently reliable conclusion | No terms, risk authorization, or submission |

Operational interruption is distinct from completed model `ABSTAIN`. Interrupted
research artifacts may remain durable for inspection, but cannot enter replay.
Storage failure, invalid/corrupt/missing frozen artifacts, provenance or temporal
invariant failure, provider/workflow interruption, research-budget exhaustion,
external cancellation, and comparable operational failures abort evaluation.
They never become `NO_TRADE`, model `ABSTAIN`, ordinary skipped candidates, or
successful failure counts. Existing exceptions/cancellation propagate; replay adds
candidate/run/boundary diagnostic context to research failures, with no automatic
retry, tolerant continuation, or crash resume. Earlier committed research remains
inspectable even when replay returns no result. Deterministic terms/risk/entry
rejections and the existing holding-gap behavior remain ordinary financial outcomes.

### Fresh budget and identity

One unit of `max_research_runs` is reserved for every admitted fresh candidate
before journal registration or model inference, regardless of eventual outcome,
terms rejection, or portfolio state. If the next candidate would exceed the cap,
`ResearchBudgetExceeded` raises before beginning its research. No silent truncation
produces apparently complete metrics. The existing per-run workflow model-call,
tool, input/output, and deadline budgets also apply. These are software limits,
not a provider billing guarantee.

Keep identities distinct:

- Replay `run_id` hashes the input manifest.
- Fresh `trial_id` is caller-supplied and differentiates fresh trials.
- `opportunity_id` is scanner/runtime identity, not a hash of all research context.
- `research_run_id(replay_run_id, opportunity)` hashes purpose
  `replay_research_invocation_v1`, originating replay ID, opportunity ID, and
  canonical opportunity-context digest.
- Model call IDs are deterministic local IDs within that research run.
- Evidence IDs identify normalized contributing evidence under the research contract.

Research settings/contracts live in
[replay_research.py](../src/crypteo/domain/replay_research.py).
`ResearchReplayManifest` retains code, Python/Pydantic versions, full `ReplayConfig`,
dataset identities and matching source bindings, research settings, and research
policy identity. It binds scanner, trade-terms, risk, execution, admission, and
failure policies. Research policy contains the workflow and market-research
versions, configuration digest, prompt and structured-schema identities for each
model stage, and tool-definition identities per subject pair. Validation requires
the currently implemented policy identities.

Fresh semantic identity depends on configured inputs, including requested model
configuration, trial and cap, not generated conclusions. Returned provider IDs,
usage, raw observations, actual model replies, provider/SDK and graph execution
metadata belong to durable research provenance rather than fresh replay input
identity. There is no circular dependency on artifacts produced during fresh replay.
Repeating an identical fresh manifest derives the same research-run IDs; duplicate
store registration rejects before inference. Use an explicit distinct trial for a
new retained fresh trial. Fresh inference need not yield identical conclusions.

### Frozen mapping and originating compatibility

Each `FrozenResearchMapping` contains `opportunity_id`, `research_run_id`,
`artifact_checksum`, `context_digest`, and `origin`. Mappings are sorted by
opportunity ID and require unique opportunity and research-run IDs. Successful
frozen replay requires exactly the admitted candidate set: missing mappings fail
on candidate admission, and unused mappings fail completed-result validation.
There is no heuristic journal search for a similar opportunity or assessment.

`FreshReplayOrigin` retains originating fresh `replay_run_id`, `code`, `trial_id`,
and `max_research_runs`. Compatibility reconstructs the originating fresh manifest
from the current research manifest's deterministic/configuration inputs, replacing
frozen settings with fresh settings containing the retained trial and cap. Its
canonical identity must equal the retained fresh replay ID; originating code must
equal current replay code. This binds the full original manifest without copying
it into every compact record. The expected research-run ID is then derived from
that originating fresh replay ID and the current canonical opportunity context.
The artifact's producer `CodeIdentity` must equal the origin code.

Under this strict V1 contract, changed code identity (commit or source digest),
interval, starting capital, assets/datasets/acquisition/source bindings, risk,
execution/fees/holding settings, market increments, terms configuration, policy,
research configuration, or other manifest-bound inputs reject frozen reuse.
Python/Pydantic identities also participate in manifest reconstruction. Frozen
settings reference already-produced artifacts; their own replay ID differs from
the originating fresh ID. This is a reproducibility/provenance contract, not a
portable assessment cache for arbitrary changed experiments. Integrity linkage
validates represented relationships, not cryptographic authenticity against a
party able to rewrite data and recompute hashes.

### Durable artifact admission and external links

`ResearchStore.load_terminal()` remains the journal/artifact validation authority
specified under [durable loading](#validation-on-inspection-and-load). Replay does
not duplicate its full lifecycle/checksum validator. After loading, replay adds:

- Exact expected research-run ID and agreement between artifact and retained request.
- Canonical full `Opportunity` context association via
  `opportunity_context_digest`, with purpose `replay_opportunity_context_v1` and
  deterministic replay serialization. This binds opportunity ID, venue, pair,
  timeframe, trigger type/payload, summary, observation and availability context.
  Python object equality is not authoritative: Python-equal but canonically
  different payloads cannot be substituted.
- `context.research_at == opportunity.observed_at == T` and
  `opportunity.available_at <= T`, matching the replay record boundary.
- Exact source bindings and workflow configuration matching the manifest;
  current manifest workflow/policy/prompt/schema/tool/market-research identities.
  The durable validator owns retained request/stage/tool relationships; replay
  association is an additional admission boundary, not an independent reinference.
- Terminal `COMPLETED` status with `MODEL` assessment origin.
- Fresh origin matching the current manifest, or frozen origin/mapping compatibility,
  including expected context digest, research-run ID, and artifact checksum.
- Artifact producer code matching originating replay code. Terminal sequence and
  ordered journal digest are retained from the validated artifact.

Pure `ReplayResult` validation cannot prove that external journal artifacts exist
or that compact projections match them. For imported research results used in
evaluation, `validate_research_links(result, *, store)` revalidates the result,
resolves every referenced research run through `load_terminal`, reapplies admission
against the admitted opportunity and historical boundary, and compares the entire
compact projection with the loaded artifact. `load_research_replay(encoded, *, store)`
combines JSON parsing with this external validation. These helpers perform no
inference, research-tool dispatch, or research market-data reads. They do not
reconstruct market signals/execution from unretained candle paths.

### Temporal ownership and execution

Fresh research uses the replay's exact `SimulationClock` and
`PointInTimeDataGateway`. At entry `opportunity.observed_at == T`,
`opportunity.available_at <= T`, and `gateway.now == T`. Awaiting model inference
does not advance the clock. After fresh durable publication/loading or frozen
artifact loading/admission, replay checks that the gateway remains at T before
financial preparation; the shared lifecycle checks it again when selection returns.

Models cannot select an arbitrary historical cutoff, timestamp, subject pair,
venue, or lookback/window through research tools. Terms, risk, broker, accounting,
and replay remain deterministic and point-in-time bounded. Submission at T is
eligible for the open of `[T, T+1h)`; the fill is reconciled only when that candle
is completed and visible, normally at T+1h. Wall-clock inference latency changes
neither eligibility nor simulated chronology. Existing missing-entry, gap,
affordability, stop/target/time-exit and final-boundary semantics are unchanged.

The gateway excludes future market DATA from deterministic tools. Crypteo cannot
prove historical purity of an LLM's parametric knowledge; a frozen data boundary
does not establish it, in standalone or replayed research. Fresh historical replay
is auditable and point-in-time data-controlled, but must not be assigned the same
historical-purity guarantee as a purely deterministic strategy. Frozen artifacts
reproduce retained conclusions; they do not solve that knowledge limitation.

### Compact records and mode-aware validation

A `RESEARCH` record contains `ReplayResearchRecord`: `opportunity_id`,
`context_digest`, `research_run_id`, `artifact_schema_version`, `artifact_checksum`,
`terminal_sequence`, `ordered_journal_digest`, `research_at`, `status`,
`assessment_origin`, `outcome`, optional `confidence`, `policy_digest`, and `origin`.
`status` is restricted to `COMPLETED`, `assessment_origin` to `MODEL`, and
artifact schema to `"1"`.
`TRADE`/`NO_TRADE` require integer confidence 0–100; model `ABSTAIN` may omit it.
Full thesis, critique, evidence, provider observations, complete model-call history,
and journal events remain owned by `ResearchArtifact`/journal and are not duplicated
into `ReplayResult`. Financial progression derives from the actual record chain,
without redundant mutable preparation/submission flags.

Every admitted research candidate requires exactly its associated research record.
For `TRADE`, valid downstream grammar is `RESEARCH → TERMS_REJECTION`, or
`RESEARCH → TERMS → RESEARCH_SELECTION → RISK_REJECTION`, or
`RESEARCH → TERMS → RESEARCH_SELECTION → RISK_AUTHORIZATION → SUBMISSION`.
`NO_TRADE`/model `ABSTAIN` end with `RESEARCH`; financial preparation/submission
is forbidden. Boundary observations follow all candidate chains. Research run IDs
cannot be reused across candidates; fresh counts cannot exceed the cap; frozen
mapping coverage must be exact.

Research and financial records must match the same candidate, opportunity context,
market, configuration, and historical boundary. Existing ledger-prefix, timing,
order, valuation and final-result checks remain enforced. `ReplayResult` and directly
related nested contracts revalidate retained instances as well as dict/JSON inputs;
passing existing instances or unchecked `model_copy()` results back through those
validation boundaries cannot bypass nested research invariants. Both financial
and research metrics are recomputed from authoritative retained records.

### Research metrics and reproduction

Financial `ReplayMetrics` remain unchanged. Research mode additionally requires
`research_metrics: ResearchReplayMetrics`; baseline has `None` and omits that field
from serialization. Actual fields and meanings are:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `opportunities_assessed` | Admitted validated research records |
| `trade_count` | Model `TRADE` outcomes |
| `no_trade_count` | Model `NO_TRADE` outcomes |
| `abstain_count` | Completed model `ABSTAIN` outcomes |
| `terms_rejection_count` | Deterministic terms rejections after `TRADE` |
| `risk_rejection_count` | Deterministic risk rejections after `TRADE` |
| `risk_rejection_reasons` | Reason-sorted tuple of `reason`/positive `count` records |
| `submission_count` | Authorized broker submissions |
| `entry_count` | Actual `ENTRY` fills |
| `research_to_entry_conversion` | Entries / opportunities assessed |
| `trade_to_entry_conversion` | Entries / model `TRADE` outcomes |

Ratios are fractions, rounded half-even to 50 significant digits, and `None` when
the denominator is zero. These factual counts/ratios do not measure accuracy,
alpha, significance, confidence calibration, profitability probability, or cost.
Operational failures abort rather than accumulate successful failure metrics.

Fresh replay is auditable through input identity and durable artifacts, but repeated
inference can differ even with identical market inputs. Frozen replay performs zero
new research inference and can be deterministic/byte-stable for identical mapping,
validated artifacts, deterministic replay inputs, and implementation/provenance
assumptions. Frozen reproduction consumes terminal research artifacts rather than
rerunning stored model responses through the graph. Neither mode resolves the
[parametric-knowledge limitation](#temporal-ownership-and-execution). Shared
economics/configuration support the
[baseline/research comparison](#baselineresearch-comparison); experiment
management is future work.
[ADR-011](decisions/ADR-011-model-research-historical-replay.md) records the rationale.

### Baseline/research comparison

[evaluation.py](../src/crypteo/evaluation.py) pairs completed results; contracts
live in [domain/evaluation.py](../src/crypteo/domain/evaluation.py). The unit is
one window that admits exactly one scanner candidate, replayed once with
`run_scanner_only` and once with `run_research_replay` (fresh or frozen) under
identical `ReplayConfig`, datasets and code
([ADR-013](decisions/ADR-013-v2-evaluation-scope.md)). The comparison runs no
replay, inference, research-tool dispatch, or market-data read.

`compare_replays(pairs, *, store)` takes an iterable of `(baseline, research)`
results, sorts windows into canonical order, and returns a `ResearchComparison`.
`load_comparison(encoded, *, store)` parses canonical JSON. Both call
`validate_research_links` for every research result: pure contract validation
cannot prove that referenced research artifacts exist.

| Contract | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| `FinancialOutcome` | `status`; `exit_reason` and exact `net_pnl` only for `CLOSED` |
| `CandidateComparison` | `opportunity_id`, `pair`, `observed_at`, research `outcome` and `confidence`, `baseline` and `research` financial outcomes |
| `WindowComparison` | Baseline `ReplayResult`, research `ReplayResult`, `candidate` |
| `ComparisonMetrics` | Descriptive aggregates below |
| `ResearchComparison` | Ordered `windows`, `metrics`, `comparison_id` |

Financial status is the candidate's terminal result in that mode: `NOT_SELECTED`
(research `NO_TRADE`/`ABSTAIN`), `TERMS_REJECTED`, `RISK_REJECTED`,
`ENTRY_REJECTED`, `CLOSED` (exit `STOP`/`TARGET`/`TIME` with the `ClosedTrade` net
P&L), `OPEN_AT_END`, or `DATA_GAP` (unresolved holding gap).

`WindowComparison` validation, on every parse:

1. Revalidates both results.
2. Requires a schema `"1"` `SCANNER_ONLY` baseline and a schema `"2"` `RESEARCH`
   result; swapped or same-mode pairs fail.
3. Requires equal `code`, `python_version`, `pydantic_version`, `replay`, and
   `datasets`. Scanner-policy strings differ by design and are not compared.
4. Requires identical ordered `SCAN` and `DELIVERY` records.
5. Requires exactly one `ADMITTED` delivery; zero or several fail.
6. For research `TRADE`, requires the research financial projection to equal the
   baseline's: ordered records without `SCAN`/`DELIVERY`/`RESEARCH`, with
   `RESEARCH_SELECTION` mapped to its proposal, plus final portfolio and metrics.
   Order IDs are excluded from equality, but every order ID on each side must be
   the one derived from that result's `run_id` and the candidate.
7. For `NO_TRADE`/`ABSTAIN`, requires no candidate records after `RESEARCH`, zero
   entries, and a flat final portfolio at initial cash.
8. Recomputes `CandidateComparison` and requires equality.

`ResearchComparison` validation requires at least one window; unique baseline run
IDs, research run IDs, and opportunity IDs; one code identity, research policy
identity, and workflow configuration; identical `initial_cash`, `trade_terms`,
`risk`, and `execution`; and canonical order by candidate `observed_at`, then
BTC-USD → ETH-USD → SOL-USD, then `opportunity_id`. Non-canonical order is
rejected; `compare_replays` sorts before construction. `comparison_id` is the
replay-canonical identity of purpose `research_comparison_v1` and the ordered
`[baseline run_id, research run_id]` pairs. `metrics` is recomputed.

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `window_count` | Paired windows |
| `trade_count`, `no_trade_count`, `abstain_count` | Research outcomes |
| `baseline_closed_count`, `research_closed_count` | `CLOSED` outcomes per mode |
| `baseline_realized_net_pnl`, `research_realized_net_pnl` | Exact sums of `CLOSED` net P&L per mode |
| `taken` | Baseline `CLOSED` outcomes where research chose `TRADE` |
| `declined` | Baseline `CLOSED` outcomes where research chose `NO_TRADE` or `ABSTAIN` |
| `unresolved_count` | Baseline `OPEN_AT_END` or `DATA_GAP` outcomes |

`taken` and `declined` each hold exact `net_pnl`, `wins` (> 0), `losses` (< 0),
`breakevens` (= 0), and `win_rate` (wins / closed in the group, 50 significant
digits half-even, `None` when empty). Because research `TRADE` reproduces the
baseline, research realized P&L equals `taken` P&L, and baseline realized P&L
equals `taken` plus `declined`. Results from different research policies are
never pooled. Metrics are descriptive: no significance, calibration, or cost.
The comparison grants no financial authority and feeds nothing back into
research.

### Evaluation window planning

`plan_windows(datasets, settings)` in [evaluation.py](../src/crypteo/evaluation.py)
turns canonical datasets into a `WindowPlan` of single-candidate replay windows
for the comparison above. It runs no replay, inference, terms, risk or execution,
so no outcome can influence selection. `validate_plan(plan, datasets)` re-plans
from the datasets and requires exact equality.

`WindowPlanSettings` holds `purpose`, an hourly UTC `planning_start`, a strictly
positive integer `limit`, and the `initial_cash`, `trade_terms`, `risk` and
`execution` shared by every window; terms and execution price increments must
match. Purpose `EVALUATION` requires `planning_start >= EVALUATION_PERIOD_START`
(`2026-07-01T00:00:00Z`, ADR-013). Purpose `PLUMBING` accepts any start and
exists for tests and integration checks on contaminated data.

Planning validates datasets as replay does (exactly one canonical dataset per
pair, any nonempty subset of BTC-USD, ETH-USD and SOL-USD). For each pair it
builds a point-in-time gateway with the clock at that pair's last completed
candle close and scans once. A crossing depends only on the 21 candles up to its
close, and gateway visibility only grows with time. So this scan contains every
opportunity a replay could admit, identical in ID, payload, times and summary to
the one a replay starting at its `observed_at` admits. A crossing that depends on
a candle not yet available at the last close is not a candidate. Candidates with
`observed_at >= planning_start` are ordered by `observed_at`, then BTC-USD →
ETH-USD → SOL-USD, then `opportunity_id`. The planner reads each dataset's last
completed close time directly to bound windows, as planning-time inspection like
dataset integrity checks, and reads no prices outside the gateway and scanner.

The window for a candidate observed at T is `start = T`,
`end = T + max_holding_bars` hours, `assets = (pair,)`, with the settings'
economics. An authorized position enters on the candle opening at T, and the
TIME exit falls on the close of holding bar `max_holding_bars` at `end`, so by
the final reconciliation the baseline position is closed, gapped or rejected,
never open. With more than one holding bar, an `end` one hour earlier leaves it
open. A candidate observed exactly at `end` is never scanned, and earlier ones are
`NOT_CURRENT`.

Exclusions apply in this order, and the first that applies is recorded:

| Reason | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| `NOT_AVAILABLE_AT_OBSERVATION` | `available_at > observed_at`, so a replay starting at `observed_at` would not admit it |
| `WINDOW_BEYOND_DATA` | `end` is after the pair's last completed candle close |
| `OVERLAPPING_CANDIDATE` | Another scanner candidate of the same pair is observed strictly inside `(T, end)` |

The overlap rule uses scanner candidates only, including ones that are
themselves unavailable at observation. Planning walks candidates in canonical
order, selecting each one not excluded, and stops after the `limit`-th
selection. `candidates` lists every candidate from `planning_start` through that
selection; if the data runs out first, it lists all of them with fewer
selections.

`WindowPlan` holds `settings`, `scanner_policy` (the implemented scanner policy),
`datasets` (`DatasetIdentity` per supplied pair, in canonical pair order),
`candidates` and `plan_id`. Each `PlannedCandidate` holds the full `Opportunity`,
`decision` (`SELECTED` or `EXCLUDED`), `exclusion_reason` (only when excluded)
and `replay` (`ReplayConfig`, only when selected). `plan_id` is the
replay-canonical identity of the plan without `plan_id` plus purpose
`evaluation_window_plan_v1`.

Pure validation, on every parse, checks the settings rules, the scanner policy,
dataset order, candidates that are unique, canonically ordered, at or after
`planning_start` and on a planned pair, and decisions consistent with reasons
and windows. Each selected `replay` must equal the derived window.
`NOT_AVAILABLE_AT_OBSERVATION` must match the opportunity's own times. A
selected window cannot contain a listed same-pair candidate, and
`OVERLAPPING_CANDIDATE` needs a listed overlap unless the overlap could lie after
the last candidate of a plan at its limit. A plan has at most `limit`
selections and, at its limit, ends with a selection. `plan_id` is recomputed.
Dataset ends, unlisted overlaps and omitted candidates need `validate_plan`.

Limitations: single-asset windows bind only that pair's dataset, so
relative-strength evidence is unavailable to research in planned windows.
`OVERLAPPING_CANDIDATE` drops candidates followed by another crossing within the
window, which may bias the sample toward candidates whose decline did not
continue. Internal dataset gaps are not excluded; they surface as baseline
`ENTRY_REJECTED` or `DATA_GAP`.

### Evaluation runner

`await run_evaluation(plan, datasets, *, directory, store, model, configuration,
trial, max_research_runs, code=None)` in [evaluation.py](../src/crypteo/evaluation.py)
executes a plan as given and returns an `EvaluationResult`. It first runs
`validate_plan(plan, datasets)` and, before writing any file, refuses a plan with
no selected candidates, a `max_research_runs` below the number of selected
windows (such a run could never finish), and a model whose provider differs from
the configuration.
Each selected window replays only its pair's dataset, in plan order, through the
existing entrypoints and the single replay lifecycle. There is no concurrency
across windows, and the run is single-process: two runners on one directory are
unsupported.

Every file is replay-canonical JSON under `directory`:

| Path | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `plan.json` | The plan |
| `run.json` | `trial`, `max_research_runs`, workflow `configuration`, and `code` identity |
| `windows/<NNN>/baseline.json` | Scanner-only `ReplayResult`; `NNN` is the 1-based selected-window index, zero-padded to three digits |
| `windows/<NNN>/attempt-<k>.json` | `EvaluationAttempt` reserving fresh research attempt `k` |
| `windows/<NNN>/research.json` | Fresh research `ReplayResult` |
| `evaluation.json` | The final `EvaluationResult` |

`plan.json`, `run.json`, `baseline.json` and `evaluation.json` are created
exclusively. If one exists it must be byte-identical, so a resume cannot change
the plan, trial, cap, workflow configuration, code, or a baseline. Per window,
the runner:

1. Runs `run_scanner_only` with the planned `ReplayConfig` and writes or checks
   `baseline.json`.
2. If `research.json` exists, loads it with `load_research_replay` and runs no
   research. It must be canonical and come from this run's code, configuration,
   and the window's latest attempt.
3. Otherwise reserves an attempt before any inference. It counts every
   `attempt-*.json` under `windows/` and raises `ResearchBudgetExceeded`, naming
   the cap, when the count already equals `max_research_runs`. For `k > 1` it
   derives the `research_run_id` that attempt `k - 1` registered (same planned
   `ReplayConfig`, dataset, code, configuration and trial ID) and refuses if the
   store holds a `COMPLETED` run for it: that window's `research.json` must be
   restored, not replaced by fresh inference chosen after seeing a result. An
   `INTERRUPTED`, `ABORTED`, incomplete or unregistered prior attempt allows the
   retry. Otherwise it exclusively creates `attempt-<k>.json` with the window's
   next `k` and
   `trial_id = "<trial>/<opportunity_id>/<k>"`. It then runs fresh
   `run_research_replay` with `FreshResearchSettings(configuration, trial_id,
   max_research_runs=1)`. Each attempt has its own `trial_id`, so its replay
   `run_id` and `research_run_id` never collide with an earlier attempt's in the
   store. On success it writes `research.json` atomically (a temporary file in
   the same directory, then rename). On failure the exception propagates with a
   note naming the window and attempt, and the reservation stays in place.
4. Runs `compare_window(baseline, research)`, so an invariant failure stops the
   run at the window that caused it.

Every saved attempt file must be canonical and sit in its own window directory.
After all windows the runner calls `compare_replays`, builds the result from the
attempt files, and writes `evaluation.json`. A completed directory returns the
identical result without inference.

Failure semantics are V1's: operational research failure raises and never becomes
`NO_TRADE`, `ABSTAIN` or a skipped window. There are no automatic retries. A
resume is an explicit call with the same arguments. It reuses saved research and
spends a new attempt only on windows without `research.json` whose previous
attempt did not complete. The refusal also applies when research completed but
its `research.json` was never written (a crash or failed write after research);
that window then needs manual resolution, since the runner cannot rebuild a fresh
result from the store. The total across
calls never exceeds `max_research_runs`, which is a software limit, not a
billing guarantee. Results bind code identity, so a real run should start from a
clean, committed tree.

`EvaluationResult` holds `plan`, `comparison`, the base `trial`, a strictly
positive integer `max_research_runs`, ordered `attempts` (`opportunity_id`,
`attempt` ≥ 1, `trial_id`), and `evaluation_id`. Pure validation, on every
parse:

1. Comparison windows correspond one to one, in order, with the plan's selected
   candidates. Each has the same opportunity ID, baseline and research
   `ReplayConfig` equal to the planned `replay`, and the plan's dataset identity
   for its pair. The plan settings' economics equal the windows'.
2. Attempts are unique by `(opportunity_id, attempt)`, belong to selected
   candidates, and are ordered by planned window then number. Each opportunity's
   attempts are numbered 1..n without gaps, every `trial_id` is
   `"<trial>/<opportunity_id>/<attempt>"`, and the total is at most
   `max_research_runs`.
3. Each window's research record `origin.trial_id` equals its opportunity's
   latest attempt `trial_id`. Frozen research carries its fresh origin, so this
   holds for fresh and frozen results alike.
4. `evaluation_id` is the replay-canonical identity of purpose
   `evaluation_result_v1`, `plan_id`, `comparison_id`, `trial`,
   `max_research_runs` and `attempts`.

`load_evaluation(encoded, *, store)` parses canonical JSON and resolves research
links through the store.

`await reproduce_evaluation(result, datasets, *, store, code=None)` takes no
model. It resolves the original's research links through the store, validates
the plan against `datasets`, re-runs each baseline (which
must equal the saved one exactly) and replays each window's research frozen from
its saved research record, so any inference or research-tool dispatch fails. Its
comparison must have candidate comparisons, metrics, and baselines equal to the
original's. It returns an `EvaluationResult` holding the frozen research
replays, with the original attempts, trial and cap. Its research replay `run_id`s,
and so its `comparison_id` and `evaluation_id`, differ from the original's; the
records' `research_run_id`s are the original's.
Reproduction needs the same code identity, Python and Pydantic versions, since
baselines must match byte for byte and frozen research must match its origin.

## Simulation and Risk

Deterministic LONG risk authorization and sizing use `RiskService.authorize(TradeProposal, PortfolioSnapshot)`, independent of `TradeDecision`. Authorization uses current flat cash as capital and the exact gateway-visible completed candle close ending at `decision_at`, which must equal gateway time. Supplied stop/target levels must align to the price increment and satisfy `0 < stop < reference < target`; the separate trade-terms service generates them. Risk authorization also accepts direct proposals and independently validates their economics; it does not depend on a `TradeTerms` object.

Let `C` be current cash, `r` the configured risk fraction, `d` the quantity increment, and `E`/`S` the estimated entry/stop execution prices from shared adverse slippage and tick rounding. With entry/exit fee rates `f_entry`/`f_exit`, cash per unit is `A = E * (1 + f_entry)` and estimated loss per unit is `L = A - S * (1 - f_exit)`. Quantity is `q = d * min(floor(C * r / (L * d)), floor(C / (A * d)))`, computed exactly; fewer than one increment is rejected. This is the largest aligned quantity satisfying both risk and cash constraints. Authorization is nonmutating and bounds estimated nongapped stop loss, not guaranteed realized loss. Actual next-open prices never enter sizing; the broker owns gap affordability and realized execution outcomes. See [ADR-006](decisions/ADR-006-decision-time-risk-authorization.md) for the durable decision-time boundary.

`RiskService` requires strictly positive estimated net target reward per unit. Let `E` be the estimated BUY execution price from the decision-time reference and `X` the estimated SELL execution price at the aligned target, using shared adverse slippage and tick rounding. With entry/exit fee rates `entry_fee`/`exit_fee`, estimated net target reward per unit is `G = X * (1 - exit_fee) - E * (1 + entry_fee)`, and authorization requires `G > 0`; zero or negative reward yields `NON_POSITIVE_TARGET_REWARD`, after level/stop-executability checks and before capital/quantity checks. There is no epsilon or minimum reward/risk threshold. `RiskAuthorization` records the target execution estimate and net reward and recomputes them from the reference, proposal, and configuration, preventing forged diagnostics from bypassing validation. These are decision-time estimated economics, not a guarantee of positive realized target-exit P&L: actual next-open entry and opening-gap exits can differ.

Risk input validation precedes ordinary rejection: malformed proposal/portfolio
state, future portfolio history, mismatched decision time, or misaligned levels
raise. Ordinary rejection precedence is `PORTFOLIO_HALTED`, `REUSED_ORDER_ID`,
`PORTFOLIO_BUSY`, `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `INVALID_LONG_LEVELS`,
`UNEXECUTABLE_STOP`, `NON_POSITIVE_TARGET_REWARD`, `NO_CAPITAL`, then
`QUANTITY_BELOW_INCREMENT`. Authorization reserves no cash, consumes no order ID,
and advances no clock. Its public contract recomputes execution estimates and
validates alignment and maximal sizing, including existing/nested and JSON inputs.

`SimulatedBroker` consumes already-authorized LONG terms without an `Opportunity` or `TradeDecision` dependency. A decision after completed close T is eligible for the open of [T, T+1h), with the fill recorded only once that candle is gateway-visible. Market phase/time, candle availability, and recording time remain distinct; intrabar fills have no invented exact timestamp, and the broker never advances or owns a second clock. See [ADR-005](decisions/ADR-005-completed-bar-execution-reconciliation.md) for the reconciliation rationale and contract.

Entries use configurable adverse slippage and fees, validated quantity/price increments, and exact Decimal accounting. Stops and targets activate after entry. Observed opening gaps fill using the opening price and take precedence over later intrabar touches; otherwise simultaneous stop/target touches resolve stop-first for a long position. A versioned maximum holding duration counts the entry candle as holding bar one; stop/target takes priority over a time exit at the final holding bar's close. An entry candle missing or unavailable at its expected close causes terminal rejection, never rollover or revival on delayed data. Missing holding data halts financial processing with the position unresolved. The broker preserves immutable snapshots and append-only order, fill, and outcome histories within execution state; durable journaling of the financial/execution lifecycle remains unimplemented and is separate from the research journal. Future baseline comparisons must use exactly the same risk and execution configuration.

For reference price `P`, adverse slippage fraction `s`, and price increment `t`,
BUY price is `t * ceil(P * (1 + s) / t)` and SELL price is
`t * floor(P * (1 - s) / t)`, using exact increment arithmetic. A rounded
nonpositive execution price raises. Fill fee is `quantity * execution_price *
fee_rate`. Entry cash debit and cost basis include the entry fee; exit proceeds
subtract the exit fee. Gross P&L is `quantity * (exit_price - entry_price)`;
net P&L subtracts both fees. Flat cash equals initial cash plus cumulative realized
net P&L. Financial amounts are exact and independent of ambient Decimal context.

The broker permits one pending or open order and full fills only. Repeating an
identical submitted order ID is a no-op; conflicting reuse raises. New submissions
require `decision_at == gateway.now` and aligned quantity/levels. Actual next-open
entry cost above cash yields terminal `UNAFFORDABLE`, without resizing. Missing
entry data yields terminal `MISSING_ENTRY_CANDLE`. Data delayed past its expected
close counts as missing even if processing happens after it becomes visible.
Due candles process chronologically. Each processing call commits once; an
exception, including an unexecutable rounded price, leaves the prior snapshot
intact. Internal transitions and public portfolio contracts validate ledger,
position, fill, and outcome consistency.

The execution path remains simulation-only. No credentials, private keys, trading permissions, exchange adapter package, or real order path may exist.

## Research Contract Validation

`ResearchThesis`, `Critique`, and `TradeDecision` contain research content without
run/decision IDs, authoritative cutoffs, or runtime/graph/journal timestamps.
Runtime associates those records with context; the model cannot maintain linkage.
`Opportunity` and `EvidenceItem` retain deterministic IDs for references.

Common research models are frozen, reject undeclared fields, revalidate existing
and nested instances, and detach mutable collections into deeply immutable state.
Evidence requires a nonempty structured payload or an artifact reference; prose
alone is insufficient. Evidence references must be nonblank and unique within
each reference collection. These common schemas check local relationships. The
workflow and retained `ResearchRunResult` additionally require references to resolve to successful
evidence collected in that run.

`TRADE` requires supporting evidence IDs, confidence, and a critic response;
`NO_TRADE` requires counterevidence IDs, confidence, and a critic response. Neither
permits abstention reasons. `ABSTAIN` requires at least one abstention reason and
may retain available research results. Confidence is a strict integer from 0 to
100 expressing confidence in the research conclusion, not a calibrated probability
of profit. There is no deterministic decision threshold. Every completed
model assessment, including `ABSTAIN`, also requires a nonblank critic response;
runtime interruption assessments follow the failure semantics below.

## Bounded Model-backed Research

The async `run_opportunity_research(request, service, model, configuration, *, code=None)`
API in [research_workflow.py](../src/crypteo/research_workflow.py) returns an
in-memory `ResearchRunResult`. It is research-only: no risk authorization,
trade-term calculation, execution, portfolio mutation, or P&L/accounting occurs.
[ADR-009](decisions/ADR-009-bounded-model-directed-research.md) records the durable
choice; [workflow contracts](../src/crypteo/domain/research_workflow.py) define
validated inputs, limits, results, and trace records.

### Entry and temporal binding

Entry is opportunity-led only. `OpportunityResearchRequest` requires an explicit
nonblank `research_run_id` and validated `Opportunity`; no run ID is generated by
the model. Subject, venue, and timeframe derive from the opportunity. Runtime
captures one `T = service.now` from the same executing `MarketResearchService`,
with its source bindings. T must be an hourly UTC boundary;
`Opportunity.observed_at == T` and `Opportunity.available_at <= T` are required.
Invalid entry/context raises before inference. There is no caller-selectable
cutoff or lookback, and the model cannot choose or change T.

Time is checked around model, tool, and cache boundaries and before return.
Moving the authoritative clock raises an invariant error, including movement
while awaiting inference. Deterministic evidence remains subject to
`PointInTimeDataGateway` visibility and the fixed-window research service.
Model inference remains subject to the
[parametric-knowledge limitation](#temporal-ownership-and-execution).

### Investigation policy and graph

The runtime-enforced policy is `bounded_research_v1`. Budget and operational
limits are validated literal configuration values, not freely tunable overrides.
Exactly seven deterministic
capabilities are exposed: `trend`, `momentum`, `volatility`, `bollinger_position`,
`levels`, `volume_context`, and `relative_strength`. Runtime binds the subject;
the six single-asset tools accept empty arguments. Relative-strength `benchmark`
is the only model-selectable research argument. Permitted benchmarks are the
other two supported BTC/ETH/SOL USD pairs, in deterministic BTC, ETH, SOL order.
They are offered even if a source is absent; the service then returns typed
unavailability. Venue, timeframe, cutoff, and windows are runtime-owned.

| Bound | Current policy |
| --- | --- |
| Admitted tool attempts | At most 8 per run |
| Tool requests per investigation response | Exactly 1 for a tool response; completion requests none |
| Investigation model calls | At most 9 |
| Thesis, critic, final assessment calls | Exactly 1 each in a completed run |
| Total model calls | At most 12 |
| Invalid tool requests | First permits recovery; second interrupts |
| Automatic provider and graph retries | 0 |
| Graph recursion limit | 24, a defensive guard rather than the primary budget |

Admitted invalid requests, unavailable results, and cache hits consume attempts.
Identical bound queries are cached within a run, including unavailable results;
cache hits retain linkage to an earlier result and do not dispatch again.
Malformed/unknown/extra arguments or multiple tool requests dispatch nothing and
produce typed rejection feedback. The first invalid request allows another
investigation response within remaining budgets. The second interrupts. A tool
request after all admitted attempts are consumed interrupts with
`TOOL_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`; the investigator may instead complete on its ninth call.

The graph sequence is
`investigate --tool request--> execute_tool --result--> investigate`;
only investigation completion advances through
`investigate --complete--> draft_thesis → critique → final_assessment`.
Investigation returns either a tool request or structured completion containing
summary, relevant evidence IDs, and remaining gaps. The investigation instructions
require the summary to be a nonblank synthesis of the evidence collected so far,
including on a completion forced at the budget boundary (`attempts_remaining` is
zero); a blank summary fails validation as `INVALID_MODEL_OUTPUT` and is never
filled by runtime. Synthesis stages have no tools.
A completed run cannot skip critic; interruptions terminate without requiring
later stages to execute. Outputs must contain exactly the declared structured
schema fields; the runtime does not repair malformed output or fill missing fields.

### Model boundary and operational limits

`ResearchModel` owns provider identity, preparation of the provider-facing JSON,
and async invocation returning typed replies. `AnthropicResearchModel` is the
research implementation, using the Claude Messages API with structured JSON-schema
output, strict tools, and parallel tool use disabled. The caller supplies an
`AsyncAnthropic` client, owns its lifetime, and explicitly selects the model.
`ModelParameters` holds explicit supported parameters only: `effort` is one of
`low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, or `max`, or unset. A set effort is sent as
`output_config.effort`; an unset effort is omitted, so the provider default
applies. Unknown levels and any other parameter, including `temperature`, are
rejected by contract validation.

The Claude adapter:

- Sends the exact prepared UTF-8 bytes rather than independently rendering
  another body.
- Enforces zero SDK retries and a 180-second client timeout even for an injected
  client; there are no workflow retries.
- Drops any client middleware, which runs inside the SDK retry loop and could
  rewrite, resend, or substitute a reply.
- Omits every client- and environment-supplied `anthropic-beta` header, so no
  beta feature such as server-side model fallback applies. Only the SDK's own
  authentication marker for OAuth or workload-identity credentials, added at
  authentication time, can still carry that header.
- Pins `anthropic-version: 2023-06-01` per request, overriding client default
  headers and `ANTHROPIC_CUSTOM_HEADERS`.
- Requires the reply's `model` to equal the requested `model` from the retained
  request body. A mismatch is an invalid-envelope failure ("Claude returned a
  different model") for every stop reason, including refusal and `max_tokens`; a
  reply whose request body is missing or unparseable is an invalid-envelope
  failure ("Claude reply lacks its request"). Another model's reply never becomes
  research output.
- Is stateless: each request carries the full accumulated context, and returned
  thinking blocks are neither interpreted nor replayed to later calls. Claude's
  thinking tokens count against the output caps below.
- Removes constraint keywords that Claude structured outputs reject (`minimum`,
  `maximum`, `exclusiveMinimum`, `exclusiveMaximum`, `multipleOf`, `minLength`,
  `maxLength`, `minItems`, `maxItems`, `uniqueItems`) from its wire output and
  tool schemas, noting each in that subschema's description, while project
  validation still enforces them and project schema identities are unchanged.

| Limit | Current value |
| --- | --- |
| Per-model-call deadline | 180 seconds |
| Overall workflow deadline | 900 seconds |
| Investigation output cap | 8,192 output tokens per call |
| Thesis/critic/assessment output cap | 16,384 output tokens per call |
| Provider-facing request | At most 128 KiB (131,072 UTF-8 bytes) per call |

The input limit applies to the exact prepared credential-free provider request
body, including messages, schemas, tool definitions, and provider parameters;
internal audit-only fields do not count. A body above the limit is retained as a
request rejection and is not transmitted. Both adapters send those same prepared
UTF-8 bytes rather than independently rendering another body.

Software uses monotonic deadlines; each inference wait is bounded by the earlier
of its per-call deadline and the remaining workflow deadline. Late model replies
are never accepted, even if the provider returns after suppressing cancellation.
Overdue tasks are cancelled and eventual completion/exceptions observed without
awaiting cancellation cooperation, so suppression cannot extend normal workflow
control indefinitely. The task may outlive the workflow; cancellation is not a
promise of provider-side termination. External cancellation propagates rather
than returning an ordinary research assessment. Synchronous preparation/tool
work is checked at boundaries, not forcibly preempted by the overall deadline.

### Evidence and independent critique

Only successful runtime-produced `EvidenceItem`s are citeable. Typed unavailable
results are retained as missing evidence, not converted into `EvidenceItem`s.
Collected evidence IDs are deduplicated with full-content consistency: an ID
repeated with different content raises. Presentation order is capability order
(trend, momentum, volatility, Bollinger position, levels, volume context, relative
strength), then benchmark order, then evidence ID. Exact deterministic structured
values are preserved in model context; Decimal values remain strings rather than
being rounded or converted to binary floats. Raw candle datasets stay outside
workflow state.

Investigation, thesis, and final assessment evidence references must belong to
successful evidence collected in that run. Critique is structured textual content
without separate citation fields. The critic is a separate fresh model call and
context receiving the exact validated thesis, all collected evidence, and tool
request history including unavailability. It challenges unsupported claims,
contradictions, missing evidence, assumptions, and interpretive overreach. It does
not inherit the investigator's completion narrative or a provider conversation.
Independence is procedural only; statistical independence is not claimed.
Final assessment receives the validated thesis and critique and must address the
critic explicitly for every completed outcome.

### Outcomes and failure semantics

`TradeDecision` retains its research vocabulary:

- `TRADE`: research supports further consideration of the LONG setup.
- `NO_TRADE`: research supports rejecting it.
- `ABSTAIN`: a reliable research conclusion could not be formed.

`TRADE` is not deterministic authorization, position sizing, trade-term generation,
or order submission. Confidence, when present, is the existing strict 0–100
self-assessment of the research conclusion. There is no deterministic confidence
threshold, calibration guarantee, or probability-of-profit claim.

| Run status | Assessment origin | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `COMPLETED` | `MODEL` | Investigation completion, validated thesis, critique, and final assessment; no failure |
| `INTERRUPTED` | `RUNTIME` | Typed failure and deterministic runtime `ABSTAIN` with matching abstention category |

A completed model `ABSTAIN` has passed all research stages and contains a critic
response. Runtime `ABSTAIN` represents an operational interruption and may retain
only the stages already produced. It is not a completed model research conclusion.
Provider failure must never become `NO_TRADE`.

Typed operational failures distinguish provider network/HTTP errors, model timeout,
overall workflow timeout, refusal, incomplete output, malformed provider envelope
or model output, unknown evidence references, invalid-tool policy, and tool/model/
input budget exhaustion. Envelope/output/reference and invalid-tool failures map
to `VALIDATION_FAILURE`; exhausted budgets map to `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`; provider,
timeout, refusal, and incomplete failures map to `RESEARCH_FAILURE`. These are
runtime abstention reasons, not financial or substantive rejection outcomes.

Clock movement, corrupt deterministic results, programmer errors, and invariant
failures raise rather than becoming ordinary research conclusions. Unexpected
provider-adapter exceptions also raise; only typed operational replies are
converted into interruption results.

### In-memory audit and retained-result validation

Current retention includes research-run identity, opportunity/context, T and source
bindings; code identity, workflow configuration, provider adapter/SDK identity and
LangGraph version; deterministic local model-call IDs (`<run>/model/0001`, etc.);
prompt/schema/tool-definition versions and digests; exact observable model
requests and responses; requested model and parameters, returned model identity
and available provider metadata (request/response IDs, status, usage, and sanitized
error metadata); tool requests/results/rejections/cache linkage; investigation,
thesis, critique, assessment; and transitions/termination. Bodies are retained when
observable; network failures/timeouts do not invent a response body. Credentials,
arbitrary headers, SDK objects, and hidden chain of thought are not trace content.

The in-memory entrypoint retains this audit content without persistence. The
[durable entrypoint](#durable-research-lifecycle) additionally commits lifecycle
boundaries and opaque provider observations. Exact fresh-inference reproducibility
is not promised; repeated inference may yield different research under identical inputs.

Retained-result validation requires repeated evidence IDs to have identical full
content; local IDs to belong to the enclosing run; tool subjects/queries, research
times, and source relationships to match context; cache links to resolve to earlier
identical queries/results; and model request contexts, evidence collections, and
request histories to match the accumulated trace. Output references resolve to
that run's evidence, and status/origin/failure/stage relationships are validated.
Retained trace and context cannot be arbitrarily recombined into a valid result.
These guarantees validate represented relationships, not authenticity of external
market data or regeneration of model inference from an artifact.

## Durable Research Lifecycle

### Durable entrypoint and ownership

`run_opportunity_research` remains a valid in-memory API returning
`ResearchRunResult`. The async durable API in
[research_workflow.py](../src/crypteo/research_workflow.py) is
`run_durable_opportunity_research(request, service, model, configuration, *, store, code=None)`.
It requires a `ResearchStore` and an `ObservableResearchModel` supporting observation
before project decoding; `AnthropicResearchModel` implements this boundary. It uses
the same bounded opportunity-led workflow, temporal bindings, and research policy.
The caller/runtime explicitly supplies `research_run_id`. Registration commits
the header and `RUN_STARTED` together; duplicate run IDs reject before inference,
including IDs belonging to incomplete or terminal runs.

`ResearchStore(path, *, lock_wait_ms=1000)` in
[research_persistence.py](../src/crypteo/research_persistence.py) uses a caller-owned
local SQLite database through Python's standard library, with no new persistence
dependency. The parent directory must already exist and be writable; the store
creates an empty database/schema when needed. It supplies one writer capability
per newly registered run. There is no reopen-for-append API, takeover, or lease.
Distinct runs may proceed concurrently, including across local processes; SQLite
serializes write transactions, so concurrency does not guarantee freedom from
contention failures. Callers must serialize use of a given run's writer.

### Storage and durability

The database schema uses `PRAGMA user_version=1` and versioned run, event, and
artifact tables. Headers/event envelopes use `schema_version="1"`; artifacts use
`artifact_schema_version="1"`. The store checks the expected schema and constraints
on transactions and rejects unsupported versions or altered schemas without
automatic migration. Each transaction owns its connection; writes use
`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, reads use `BEGIN`.

Every connection configures and verifies:

| PRAGMA | Enforced value |
| --- | --- |
| `journal_mode` | `DELETE` (rollback journal) |
| `synchronous` | `EXTRA` (`3`) |
| `foreign_keys` | `ON` |
| `busy_timeout` | `lock_wait_ms` |
| `fullfsync` | `ON` on macOS |

Lock waiting defaults to 1,000 ms and accepts integer values from 0 through
60,000 ms. Failure to establish the configured settings fails closed. `EXTRA`
requests rollback-journal synchronization including directory synchronization
after journal deletion; macOS additionally requests full filesystem synchronization.
Transactional recovery and durability rely on SQLite and a local filesystem/storage
stack honoring locking and synchronization. This is not a distributed durability
guarantee or protection against arbitrary filesystem/hardware failure.

### Events and lifecycle states

The closed lifecycle vocabulary is defined by the
[journal contracts](../src/crypteo/domain/research_journal.py):

| Event | Retained boundary |
| --- | --- |
| `RUN_STARTED` | Registered run header, context, configuration, and version identities |
| `MODEL_REQUEST_PREPARED` | Normalized request and prepared provider-facing JSON |
| `MODEL_REQUEST_REJECTED` | Prepared request rejected before invocation for input budget or workflow deadline |
| `MODEL_CALL_STARTED` | Local call ID admitted before entering the provider |
| `MODEL_RESPONSE_OBSERVED` | Exact client-visible HTTP entity bytes, status, request ID, and encoding |
| `MODEL_CALL_FINISHED` | Request paired with normalized project-owned `ModelReply` |
| `TOOL_REQUESTED` | Attempt ID, producing model-call ID, and original tool requests |
| `TOOL_RESOLVED` | Bound query, result or rejection, admission, and cache linkage |
| `STAGE_COMPLETED` | Validated investigation, thesis, critique, or assessment and producing call ID |
| `NODE_TRANSITION` | Graph source/target and any termination reason |
| `RUN_FINISHED` | Completed/interrupted status, assessment origin, assessment, and failure if present |
| `RUN_ABORTED` | Restricted cancellation or invariant/programmer failure diagnostic |

Inspection exposes four durable states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `INCOMPLETE` | Valid committed lifecycle prefix with no terminal event; no terminal artifact |
| `COMPLETED` | `RUN_FINISHED` with completed model research and a terminal artifact |
| `INTERRUPTED` | `RUN_FINISHED` with typed operational failure, runtime `ABSTAIN`, and a terminal artifact |
| `ABORTED` | `RUN_ABORTED` closes the history without manufacturing a `ResearchRunResult` or ordinary `ResearchArtifact` |

`INCOMPLETE` does not prove a process crashed: work may be active, deliberately
stopped, or unable to record another boundary. A completed model `ABSTAIN` remains
`COMPLETED`, distinct from operational `INTERRUPTED` research.

Events have journal-owned contiguous sequence numbers starting at zero. Terminal
events permanently close a run. Schema triggers and lifecycle validation reject
`UPDATE`, `DELETE`, replacement of immutable rows, duplicate event submission,
duplicate registrations, and events after terminal closure. A terminal artifact
cannot be silently replaced. Each append checks that the committed registration
and prefix still match the writer's retained history.

### Write order and external effects

Successful commits establish these ordering guarantees:

1. `RUN_STARTED` persists before inference.
2. The prepared model request persists before invocation. Input-size/deadline
   rejection is retained without invoking that request.
3. `MODEL_CALL_STARTED` persists before provider invocation. Runtime checks the
   deadline after that synchronous commit and again immediately inside the
   scheduled provider coroutine, with no yield before entering the provider.
   A call-start record therefore does not prove the provider was entered.
4. Provider entity bytes persist before project decoding and normalization.
5. Tool request persists before dispatch or cache resolution; resolution persists
   before downstream use.
6. Validated stage output persists before advancing to its dependent stage.
7. `RUN_FINISHED` and the validated `ResearchArtifact` publish in one transaction.

No transaction spans SQLite and the external provider, nor does one span tool
execution. A committed start/request may have an unresolved external outcome;
the journal cannot establish exactly-once external effects. Observation callbacks
close at deadline/cancellation, so late responses cannot append to closed history.
A response observed and committed before the software deadline may still precede
a normalized timeout if the call finishes too late.

### Exact provider observation

The Claude adapter's observable invocation exposes both successful and
HTTP-error response entities before project decoding. `MODEL_RESPONSE_OBSERVED`
retains the exact bytes separately as a SQLite BLOB, including malformed content.
Here "exact" means HTTP entity bytes observable by the client, not TLS records or
wire framing. Network failures/timeouts do not invent a body when none was
observed.

Project decoding is strict UTF-8, independent of a conflicting charset header.
Malformed UTF-8 remains opaque retained bytes and cannot become replacement-character
model content; a malformed success entity normalizes to an invalid-envelope failure.
Project JSON decoding rejects array/object nesting deeper than a fixed maximum of
256 as invalid JSON before parsing, so provider bodies, tool arguments, structured
output, and stored bodies decode identically on every supported Python version.
Normalized project-owned `ModelReply` and metadata remain distinct from the raw
observation. Neither layer claims to capture hidden model reasoning, credentials,
arbitrary headers, or SDK objects.

### Failure semantics

Journal/database creation failure, duplicate run ID, append/lock/commit or
synchronization failure, uncertain commit outcome, unsupported schema/version,
and stored corruption/validation failure raise `ResearchStorageError` from
[research_journal.py](../src/crypteo/research_journal.py). Persistence failures
stop further workflow work and return no terminal artifact to the caller. They
never become substantive `NO_TRADE` or ordinary runtime `ABSTAIN`.

A failed or uncertain write invalidates the writer capability, preventing further
appends/publication. A commit acknowledgement can fail after SQLite committed:
later validated inspection may find that event, or even a fully published terminal
artifact, although the failing invocation returned none. The runtime does not
retry, infer rollback, or continue inference on that uncertain history.

Unexpected programmer/invariant failures preserve their original exception
identity; they are not broadly translated into persistence errors or research
conclusions. External cancellation propagates. If the journal remains healthy,
`RUN_ABORTED` recording is best effort and cannot hide the original error or
cancellation. It retains only the restricted diagnostic category. Persistence
failure itself does not attempt an abort on an unhealthy writer.

### Serialization and integrity

Project records use deterministic UTF-8 JSON: Pydantic JSON-mode values, sorted
keys, compact separators, unescaped valid Unicode, and no nonfinite JSON constants.
Invalid Unicode surrogates and duplicate JSON keys are rejected on project JSON
loading. Decimal fields use exact strings without conversion through binary floats;
their retained Decimal spelling can preserve trailing zeros or exponent notation
(for example `"1.2300"` or `"1E+3"`). UTC datetimes use RFC 3339 `Z`, omitting
fractional seconds when zero and using six fractional digits otherwise. This
research encoding is distinct from Coinbase's canonical numeric/time encoding.
Opaque provider bodies bypass JSON encoding and retain their exact bytes.

Each event has a SHA-256 checksum over a version-labeled, length-framed envelope
and body-presence/body bytes. The terminal artifact links to the ordered digest
of all event checksums and has its own checksum excluding its checksum field.
These checks detect corruption and inconsistent retained data. They do not
authenticate history against a party able to rewrite data and recompute hashes.

### Validation on inspection and load

`store.inspect_run(research_run_id)` validates a committed snapshot and returns
`ResearchRunInspection`: header, state, events/receipts, pending boundaries, and
an artifact when present. Pending boundaries identify prepared or started calls,
observed responses awaiting resolution, requested tools, and the current
nonterminal node. `store.load_terminal(research_run_id)` returns a validated
`ResearchArtifact` for completed/interrupted runs; incomplete/aborted runs raise
because they have no terminal artifact.

Loading validates relationships as well as schemas and checksums:

- SQLite integrity/foreign keys, expected schema, canonical record encoding,
  registration versus `RUN_STARTED`, contiguous sequence, and lifecycle grammar/order.
- Run/call/attempt IDs, unique request/response pairing, stage ordering, budgets,
  and transitions consistent with retained work and termination causes.
- Tool request/query/result/rejection/cache linkage, subject, research time, and
  source bindings; evidence identity/content consistency and grounded references.
- Prepared provider JSON versus normalized request, and requests versus accumulated
  context, evidence, request history, prompts, schemas, and tool definitions.
  Provider `anthropic` is the only provider with a registered journal codec: its
  prepared body must equal the Claude adapter's pure request renderer output,
  including stripped wire schemas. Other providers compare against the
  normalized project request encoding.
- Provider observation versus normalized reply using the registered provider's
  pure decoders: every reply except a network failure or timeout requires an
  observed entity, its text encoding must be `utf-8`, an HTTP status of 400 or
  more must re-derive the normalized error, and a success entity must re-derive
  the normalized reply, including Claude's returned-model check. The permitted
  software-timeout case may supersede an observation. Providers without a codec
  cannot carry observations.
- Validated stage output versus producing reply, final `ResearchRunResult` versus
  journal projection, and terminal artifact/run/sequence/digest/checksum consistency.

Shared in-memory research validators in
[research_validation.py](../src/crypteo/research_validation.py) are reused where
appropriate. These checks validate retained relationships; they cannot establish
authenticity of external market observations. Loading performs no model inference,
market-data reads, or tool redispatch.

### Terminal artifact and recovery boundary

`ResearchArtifact` is the immutable terminal projection for `COMPLETED` or
`INTERRUPTED` research. It contains the validated `ResearchRunResult`, run ID,
terminal sequence, ordered journal digest, artifact version, and checksum. It is
validated against the journal before atomic publication with `RUN_FINISHED` and
again on load. Journal events remain authoritative; the artifact is the validated
consumer-facing projection. `ABORTED` runs have no ordinary artifact/result.

Recovery means SQLite transactional recovery, inspection of valid committed
prefixes, identification of unresolved operations, and validated terminal loading.
Automatic graph resumption, provider retry after crash, tool redispatch, conversion
of incomplete runs into `ABSTAIN`, and stale-run takeover/leases are unimplemented.
Validated terminal `ResearchArtifact` loading is the durable consumer boundary.
Historical research replay consumes it for fresh and frozen admission, then owns
deterministic terms/risk/execution after model `TRADE`. The journal itself grants
no financial authority or baseline/model comparison framework.
[ADR-010](decisions/ADR-010-durable-research-lifecycle.md) records the rationale.

## Validation Coverage

Replay artifact checks cannot reconstruct unretained market paths or prove that
externally supplied statistics came from visible candles.

The [local quality commands](../README.md#local-development) run Ruff formatting
and lint, strict mypy, and pytest. Model-backed evaluation remains separate from
normal CI; normal workflow/provider tests use mocked model responses and transports.

## Unimplemented Research and Evaluation Boundaries

Trader-led interactive research/chat, live mode, UI, specialist agents,
web/news/social/on-chain/derivatives/macro/fundamental evidence,
sentiment/tokenomics capabilities, and long-term memory/RAG remain future work.
Real exchange submission, trading credentials, private keys, and withdrawal
capabilities are out of scope.
Architecture and future entry modes are described in
[the extension boundary](architecture.md#research-extension-boundary).

Journal access is for audit, evaluation, debugging, and post-trade analysis, never
decision memory. V1 decisions do not retrieve prior theses, decisions, or outcomes.
Research replay binds scanner/risk/simulation inputs to its versioned manifest;
standalone research does not extend the schema-1 baseline manifest.

The evaluation question is whether bounded research improves selection among the
same opportunities under identical risk and execution assumptions. The
[baseline/research comparison](#baselineresearch-comparison) artifact,
[evaluation window planning](#evaluation-window-planning), and the resumable
[evaluation runner](#evaluation-runner) with frozen reproduction are implemented.
Statistics, reports, an integrated reporting harness, multi-candidate windows,
generalized experiment management, additional baselines, process/performance
graders, and statistical model-performance evaluation remain unimplemented. There is no alpha
attribution, confidence calibration, significance analysis, profitability
probability, or inference-cost estimator. Distributed replay, automatic replay
crash resumption, concurrent candidate research, tolerant continuation after
research infrastructure failure, and unrestricted frozen reuse across changed
configurations are also unimplemented.

[ADR-013](decisions/ADR-013-v2-evaluation-scope.md) defines the evaluation
periods, because the research model's training data extends through June 2026:

| Role | Hourly boundaries | Permitted use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contaminated | Before `2026-07-01T00:00:00Z` | Plumbing, determinism and integration checks only; no claims about research selection |
| Evaluation | From `2026-07-01T00:00:00Z` | Model-backed evaluation runs |

Window planning with purpose `EVALUATION` enforces the evaluation period start.
`ReplayConfig` and the comparison artifact do not enforce the periods.
